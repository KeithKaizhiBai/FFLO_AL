Reliability-aware active learning for FFLO phase spaces
Finite-momentum superconducting phase diagrams require repeated minimization over
pairing amplitude and momentum, followed by spectral tests wherever topology is meaningful.
We combine an exact Bogoliubov–de Gennes oracle with active learning that schedules, but
never replaces, these calculations. The method reconstructs the two-dimensional Normal,
uniform-superconducting, and FFLO regions with a sparse allocation of exact calculations.
Restricting topology to reliable gapped FFLO states reveals connected conventional and topo-
logical FFLO support separated by a numerically reconstructed gap-closing region. Extending
this hierarchy to(kBT/t,J A/t,µ/t) exposes systematic thermodynamic and topology-aware
structure across a finite sampled domain. The latter evidence is reported as discrete sup-
port and narrow-µbins, not as a converged continuous topological surface or complete
three-dimensional atlas.
INTRODUCTION
Superconductivity with nonzero center-of-mass pairing momentum provides a route beyond
a spatially uniform condensate. Fulde and Ferrell and, independently, Larkin and Ovchinnikov
established the finite-momentum pairing and spatially modulated mechanisms now grouped under
the FFLO setting [1, 2]. Subsequent reviews have developed the thermodynamic, material, and
ultracold-gas consequences of Fermi-surface mismatch and competing pairing momenta [3–5]. The
central phase-selection problem is therefore intrinsically thermodynamic: competing minima must
be compared over both pairing amplitude and momentum.
The search for FFLO structure spans several physical platforms. Heavy-fermion and organic
superconductors have displayed field-induced phases and spectroscopic, calorimetric, or magnetic
signatures discussed in relation to FFLO order [6–10]; these representative studies motivate the prob-
lem without implying that every identification is universal or uncontested. Population-imbalanced
ultracold Fermi gases provide a complementary tunable setting for pairing under mismatch, phase
separation, and finite-momentum phases [11–15].
Altermagnets add a distinct route to this competition. Their compensated magnetic order can
nevertheless generate symmetry-structured momentum-dependent spin splitting [16–19]. Model
studies have connected this splitting to zero-field finite-momentum or other unconventional pairing
states [20–22], and neighboring altermagnetic constructions have been proposed for topological
superconductivity and Majorana modes [23, 24]. These precedents motivate an altermagnetic
finite-q problem but do not determine the phase structure of the one-dimensional model studied
here.
Topology adds a second, logically separate requirement. Finite-momentum pairing can coexist
with topological superfluidity in spin-orbit-coupled models [25–27]. More generally, bulk sym-
metries and invariants organize topological superconducting phases [28–32]. Majorana-platform
studies connect those bulk distinctions to boundary physics [33–36]. In a gapped one-dimensional
Bogoliubov–de Gennes (BdG) system in symmetry class D, the relevantZ2 distinction can be
expressed through a Pfaffian invariant [37, 38]; its use is meaningful only after bulk-gap applicability
is established. A topology-resolved FFLO map must therefore identify a reliable thermodynamic
minimum before classifying trusted gapped FFLO states.
This hierarchy is computationally demanding because every accepted parameter point requires
minimization ofF (∆,q ), comparison with the normal reference, adaptive checks of shallow or
edge minima, and, where applicable, full-zone spectral and Pfaffian diagnostics. Active learning
and Bayesian experimental design formalize the selection of informative sequential evaluations [39–
41]; phase-diagram applications demonstrate boundary-focused sampling [42–44], while adaptive

Here we combine an exact BdG oracle with active learning to resolve the thermodynamic and
topology-aware structure of an altermagnetic finite-momentum superconductor. The oracle supplies
every physical label, while the surrogate redirects subsequent evaluations toward uncertain or
rapidly varying regions. This strategy reconstructs the two-dimensional thermodynamic phases
from a sparse subset of the prescribed candidate locations, resolves connected cFFLO and tFFLO
support within the reliable gapped FFLO sector, and exposes systematic structure across a finite
three-dimensional domain. The three-dimensional evidence remains a discrete point cloud and
narrow-µbins rather than a converged continuous topological surface.

Two-dimensional thermodynamic organization.—The exact observations in Fig. 2 organize into
a curved outer envelope separating superconducting support below from Normal support above.
At low temperature the superconducting sector reaches comparatively largeJA/t; as temperature
rises, the largest sampled splitting that still supports pairing moves downward. Most of the paired
region is finite-q FFLO. Uniform superconductivity instead forms a narrow strip close toJA = 0, so
the sampled state changes rapidly from zero- to finite-momentum pairing once the altermagnetic
splitting is introduced. This thin inner structure and the descending outer envelope are precisely the
regions where small changes of the optimized minimum can change a label, motivating concentrated
exact minimization rather than a smooth surrogate boundary.
The search begins with a sparse random design over the full(kBT/t,J A/t) domain. Subsequent
calls accumulate along both thermodynamic structures while a continuing exploration channel
samples their interiors and the remaining domain. In the primary Stage-I discovery, 6,880 unique
exact coordinates are retained from 77,361 prescribed candidate locations, corresponding to an
8.89% location fraction and an 11.24-fold reduction relative to evaluating every grid location. The
Stage-II reliability continuation adds exact evaluations without changing any shared phase label
and supplies the 7,434-coordinate endpoint shown in panel (c). The efficiency ratio is therefore a
Stage-I location count, not a wall-time acceleration or a comparison with matched random sampling.

Topology-resolved FFLO structure.—Thermodynamic FFLO order alone does not specify the
bulk topological sector. Applying the full-zone gap guard and Pfaffian invariant resolves the trusted
FFLO observations into two gapped supports (Fig. 3). The cFFLO sector occupies lowerJA/t
and extends to higher temperature, whereas the sampled tFFLO sector is shifted toward stronger
altermagnetic splitting and lower temperature. Their meeting region follows a curved locus where
the bulk gap closes before reopening with the opposite Pfaffian product. Thus the topology-resolved
map adds a bulk-state distinction that neither the position of theFimpl minimum nor the sign of
qopt can provide.

DISCUSSION
The physical result is a hierarchy of structure rather than a single phase map. The two-
dimensional exact data show a narrow uniform-SC strip, an extended finite-q region, and a curved
Normal–superconducting envelope; the spectral analysis then resolves the FFLO sector into gapped
conventional and topological bulk supports. Varying chemical potential reorganizes the coordinate
support of those states and can remove trusted tFFLO observations from an individual sampled
slice. Topology therefore supplies information that a thermodynamic FFLO label alone cannot
encode.

The location-count economy does not establish wall-time acceleration, because no matched
exhaustive timing baseline was established, and it does not establish boundary enrichment, because
boundary membership was not recorded for the complete candidate set. The stopping settings
diagnose a particular numerical workflow rather than universal physical scales. Likewise, the
three-dimensional evidence is discrete and nonuniformly sampled, so it supports higher-dimensional
exploration but neither phase volumes nor a closed continuous atlas.
The method combines adaptive sampling with direct BdG evaluation while keeping numerical
reliability separate from phase identity. The results establish finite thermodynamic and topology-
resolved sampled supports; determining continuous boundaries requires additional evidence beyond
those observations.
